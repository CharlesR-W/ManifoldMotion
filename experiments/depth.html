Motion through depth in the trained network

In the trained ResNet-20, how far does each block move the 10,000 test images in raw activation space, how much of that motion is shared by all images, and what does it do to the distances between neighbouring images?

Final checkpoint (step 64,000; test accuracy 91.56%), seed 0. Nine block steps stem→b1 … b8→b9; at the two stage changes also the shortcut alone and the whole step. Neighbourhoods: the k = 128 nearest images in the source frame. SPEC figures 2–6. Read the scale audit first: every "neighbourhood" here is as wide as the cloud.

Answer. Measured against each step's own source radius R (the cloud's RMS distance from its centroid), four steps move the cloud furthest: b8→b9 (mean squared travel 3.68 R²), stem→b1 (2.64) and the two stage-change blocks b3→b4 (1.36) and b6→b7 (1.62); the other five move it 0.27–0.71 R². (In raw units the ranking differs, because R changes from site to site.) A translation shared by all images is 12%–45% of the mean squared travel.

The same four steps stretch neighbour distances (geometric-mean factors 1.90, 1.45, 1.44 and 1.83); at b2→b3, b5→b6 and b7→b8 the geometric-mean factor stays within e±0.04. Across an image's 128 neighbour edges the stretch varies little except at b8→b9, where its RMS spread is 0.113 against at most 0.043 elsewhere. There near pairs stretch less than far ones (mean log-stretch +0.53 for the nearest neighbour, +0.68 for the farthest rank bin; stem→b1 tilts the other way, +0.68 to +0.46), and neighbourhoods are reshuffled (Jaccard overlap of the 128-neighbour sets 0.17). The network's own shortcut at a stage change shrinks neighbour distances by a geometric-mean factor of about 2 (log-stretch −0.69), nearly uniformly.

How an image's neighbours move relative to it is mostly a constant offset shared by the neighbours (51%–68% of the squared differences, averaged over images). At rank 8 a local linear fit adds little on held-out neighbours (medians over images), and the leading local principal directions capture about as much of an image's squared displacement as of another, randomly assigned image's for the first seven steps, and modestly more at the last two.

A second training seed (seed 1, test accuracy 91.52%) gives similar numbers for the last block at the final checkpoint (travel 3.56 R², mean λ +0.60, far-minus-near tilt +0.16, Jaccard 0.17) and the stage-change blocks (1.20–1.82 R²), but not the stem block (0.95 R², mean λ +0.35), so the stem block's numbers below are one seed's (sensitivity).

How far the cloud moves, and how much is a common shift

An image's displacement over a block step is $v = Y - X$, its activation after the block minus before (at a stage change, before means after the network's shortcut, $X = S\,h_l$, so both are in the same space). It is a chord over one block, not a velocity. The mean squared travel $\langle |v|^2\rangle / R_X^2$ is reported in units of the source cloud's squared radius $R_X^2 = \langle |x - \bar x|^2 \rangle$, and splits exactly into three parts:

$$ \langle |v|^2 \rangle = \underbrace{|\bar v|^2}_{\text{translation}} + \underbrace{s^2 \langle |x - \bar x|^2\rangle}_{\text{radial fit}} + \text{remainder}, \qquad s = \frac{\sum_i \langle v_i - \bar v,\, x_i - \bar x\rangle}{\sum_i |x_i - \bar x|^2}. $$

The radial-fit coefficient $s$ is the component of the displacement field along position. It is not a change of size: a rotation of a cloud within its own plane gives $s = \cos\theta - 1 < 0$ with no change of radius. The size change is $R_Y / R_X$, shown separately.

At the two stage changes the block adds new channels (the shortcut appends zeros). Of the mean squared travel of the block, 31% (b3→b4) and 40% (b6→b7) lies in the channels inherited through the shortcut; the rest is written into the appended channels.

What the blocks do to distances between neighbours

For an image $i$ and each of its 128 nearest neighbours $j$ in the source frame, the edge log-stretch is $\lambda_{ij} = \log(|y_j| / |u_j|)$, with $u_j$ and $y_j$ the offset from $i$ to $j$ before and after the step. The edge Green strain is $e_{ij} = (|y_j|^2 - |u_j|^2) / (2|u_j|^2)$ and the edge strain energy is the mean of $e^2$ over the edges. All three are defined on every edge and need no fit. A uniform rescaling by $c$ gives $\lambda = \log c$ on every edge and a strain energy of $((c^2-1)/2)^2$, so the energy counts a pure change of scale; the spread of $\lambda$ across an image's edges is what measures non-uniform change. Edges whose source or target length falls below 10−9 R are counted separately and left out of the log-stretch summaries.

How neighbours move relative to each other

The relative motion of image $i$ is $q_i = \sum_j |w_j|^2 / \sum_j |u_j|^2$ over its 128 neighbours, where $w_j = v_j - v_i$ is the difference of displacements. It measures how differently neighbours move, relative to their separation. A rigid rotation has $q > 0$ with no change of any distance, so $q$ is relative motion, not separation; the edge quantities above measure separation. The local linear fit regresses $w$ on the coordinates of the offsets $u$ in the leading m principal directions of the neighbourhood, with a free intercept, and splits $\sum|w|^2$ exactly into a common offset (the neighbours' mean relative displacement $\bar w$: a constant offset of the neighbours relative to the image), a slope share and a residual; the shares are averaged over images. Its held-out version fits on one half of the neighbours and scores on the other, both ways, against the fitting half's mean as a baseline.

Read against the fixtures on the audit page: at this radius an exactly linear map of a flat 32-dimensional cloud gives a slope share of only 26% and a held-out gain over the mean baseline of 0.16 (means over anchors); on the same mean basis the network's steps gain 0.004–0.113. A small slope share here is what a linear map would also give, so it is not evidence of a nonlinear map; it says that a neighbourhood-wide linear trend explains little of how the neighbours' displacements differ from the image's.

How the motion lines up with the neighbourhood's directions

The capture fractions take the leading m directions of the centred covariance of half of an image's neighbours (halves interleaved by rank) and ask how much of something lies in them, with the halves swapped and the results pooled or averaged: φs, the squared length of the other half's offsets, centred on the fitting half's mean (how well the directions describe unseen secants); φv, the image's own squared displacement; φw, the other half's relative displacements w. Two controls for φv: the rank-m principal plane of the whole cloud instead of the local one, and the displacement of another image, assigned by a random permutation, on this image's local plane. They are capture fractions, not a split into motion along and across the cloud.

What these numbers do and do not license

  • The neighbourhood quantities are graph quantities at the radius of 128 neighbours, whose mean distance is 1.04–1.25 times the cloud radius: "local" means "at the scale of the whole cloud", not infinitesimal. Travel, its split and the coarse predictors are whole-cloud quantities.
  • Displacements are chords over one block, not velocities, and the three stages are different spaces (a stage-change step starts from the shortcut-projected frame); mean squared travel is in units of each step's own source radius.
  • No tangent / normal component, strain tensor, curvature-type or rotation quantity is reported (see the audit). Edge strain energy counts uniform scaling; the spread of λ and the stretch profile are what show non-uniform change.
  • The raw metric is one choice among rescalings the network could absorb. Under per-channel rescaling the sign of b2→b3's small mean λ flips (−0.025 raw, +0.031 rescaled) and b1→b2's travel rises from 0.69 to 0.95 R²: these are properties of the raw metric, not invariant under channel rescaling. b8→b9 changes less (sensitivity).
  • q is relative motion, not separation. The radial-fit coefficient s is not a size change. Shares and capture fractions are descriptive; in the broad-neighbourhood fixtures an exactly linear map also gives modest slope shares and near-shuffle capture, so they do not distinguish linear from nonlinear motion.
  • Totals are means over the fixed population of the 10,000 test images (medians for held-out scores), exact for that population; the stretch profile uses 500 anchors against all images. The thinning runs on the audit page are a sensitivity to sample size, not confidence intervals.
