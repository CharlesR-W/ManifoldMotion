ManifoldMotion

The CIFAR-10 test set as a cloud of points in a ResNet-20's raw activation space, moving from block to block and over training

Open the explorer

The question

A residual network changes its representation of an image a little at each block. Put all 10,000 CIFAR-10 test images through the network and read the activations after each block: at every depth the test set is a cloud of points, and from one block to the next, and from one training checkpoint to the next, that cloud moves. The project measures the motion as the kinematics of a deforming body. How far does each image move? How much of that is a shift of the whole cloud? Which blocks change distances between neighbouring images, and which keep them? How differently do neighbours move? The original hope was also to split each image's motion into a part along the cloud and a part across it and to measure the cloud's strain; the first result below is that, at the scales 10,000 images give, no reproducible local plane was found to define that split on.

The space and the moving body

The network is a ResNet-20 trained on CIFAR-10 (seed 0; test accuracy 91.56% at the last of 40 measured checkpoints, step 64,000). Its residual stream is read at ten sites: the stem output and the outputs of blocks b1 to b9, after the ReLU, flattened. The sites of one stage share one Euclidean space; the three stages are different spaces: 16,384 dimensions for the stem and b1–b3, 8,192 for b4–b6 and 4,096 for b7–b9, all coordinates non-negative. Distances are ordinary Euclidean distances between raw activations; no readout, lens or learned metric is applied.

The material points are the test images, each with mass 1/10,000. A block step pairs an image's activation before a block, $x$, with its activation after it, $y$; the displacement $v = y - x$ is a chord over one block, not a velocity. Within a stage both frames are in the same space. At the two stage changes (into b4 and into b7) the network's own shortcut $S$ subsamples the feature map by 2 and appends zero channels; the block step is then taken from $S\,x$, and the shortcut alone and the whole step are measured separately, using only distances, because they change space. Lengths are reported in units of the source cloud's radius $R$, the RMS distance of its images from their centroid. On the training clock a step pairs one site at two consecutive checkpoints.

One caveat about the metric: within a stage, rescaling a channel by a positive factor can be undone by the next layers, so the raw Euclidean metric is one member of a family the network does not distinguish. Raw coordinates are primary. With every channel divided by its RMS over the stage, the last block's numbers barely move, but b1→b2's mean squared travel rises from 0.69 to 0.95 R² and the small mean log-stretch of b2→b3 changes sign (−0.025 raw, +0.031 rescaled); see the sensitivity figure on the audit page.

Findings

At the final checkpoint (step 64,000) unless stated, over all 10,000 test images.

  1. No anchor passes the reproducible-plane gate at the scales 10,000 images give. At ten sites, the two shortcut-projected frames and three checkpoints, for neighbourhoods of 32 to 1,024 images and planes of 2 to 32 dimensions, not one of 500 audited anchors passes the gate fixed before the activations were examined (reproducible split-half plane, eigenvalue gap, stability in radius, held-out capture). The 128th neighbour is on average 1.02–1.25 R away, intrinsic-dimension estimates are 29–79 at the smallest scale, and no (frame, k, m) cell reaches the gate's eigenvalue gap of 2 on average (some individual criteria do pass on many anchors; no anchor meets all of them at once). The tangent / normal split and the strain tensor were therefore not computed. On synthetic clouds the same procedure finds the plane of a flat 8-dimensional cloud, and no anchor passes on a flat 32-dimensional cloud or a 64-dimensional blob, whose neighbourhood scale is like the network's. Audit and calibration.
  2. Four steps move the cloud furthest, relative to its radius. Mean squared travel, in units of each step's source radius, is 3.68 R² at b8→b9, 2.64 at stem→b1, and 1.36 and 1.62 at the two stage-change blocks; the other five steps move the cloud 0.27–0.71 R². A translation shared by all images is 12%–45% of it, and most of the centred displacement lies outside the source cloud's 128 leading principal directions (only 5%–23% inside). A second training seed agrees at the last block (3.56 R²) and the stage changes, but its stem block moves the cloud only 0.95 R². Depth experiment.
  3. Most blocks rescale neighbour distances nearly uniformly within a neighbourhood; the last block does not. The four large steps stretch distances to the 128 nearest neighbours (geometric-mean factors 1.90, 1.45, 1.44, 1.83), and at b2→b3, b5→b6 and b7→b8 the geometric-mean factor stays within e±0.04. The spread of the stretch across an image's 128 edges is by far the largest at b8→b9 (RMS 0.113, against at most 0.043 elsewhere), and its 128-neighbour sets before and after overlap little (Jaccard 0.17); both hold in the second seed. Across the whole range of distances, b8→b9 stretches near pairs less than far ones (mean log-stretch +0.53 at rank 1, +0.68 in the farthest rank bin), while stem→b1 tilts more steeply the other way (+0.68 to +0.46). The network's shortcut shrinks neighbour distances by a factor of about 2, nearly uniformly. Explorer: log-stretch at b8.
  4. Neighbours mostly move by a common offset, and the local directions capture little of an image's displacement. Of the neighbours' displacements relative to an image, 51%–68% (in squared size, averaged over images) is a constant offset they share. At rank 8, the median held-out score of a local linear fit exceeds the median score of predicting that offset by at most 0.115. The 8 leading local directions capture 0.008–0.065 of an image's squared displacement on average, close to their shuffled control for the first seven blocks and modestly above it for the last two. In the broad-neighbourhood fixtures an exactly linear map can also give modest slope shares and near-shuffle capture, so these summaries do not distinguish linear from nonlinear motion. Depth experiment.
  5. Over training, the last block's reshuffling deepens. The overlap of b8→b9's neighbour sets before and after the block falls from 0.75 at initialisation to 0.38 at step 1,280 and 0.17 at the end (not monotonically). Its travel falls from 2.92 R² at initialisation to at most 0.89 between steps 32 and 1,280, then rises to 3.68. The stem block's travel peaks early (5.21 R² at step 32). Training experiment.
  6. Between checkpoints, the site-mean chords stay large until late in training, and b9 moves further than the stem. Between steps 128 and 26,880 the site-mean chord per interval is 0.28–1.04 source radii, over intervals 128 to 3,840 steps long; over the last interval (12,800 steps) it is 0.05–0.10 R. b9 travels further than the stem, relative to its radius, in 38 of 39 intervals (the sites in between are not always ordered by depth). In mid-training the site-mean straightness is below 1/√2 in 119 of 120 (interval, site) cells; a single image falls below that value only if its next chord has a backward component. Training experiment.
  7. Correctly classified images travel further at the last block. At b8→b9 they move 1.91 R against 1.58 R for the 844 misclassified ones (seed 1: 1.88 against 1.55); at earlier blocks the two groups move alike (within 4%). Depth experiment.

What was measured, and what was not

Two kinds of quantity are measured, each for every image unless stated:

  • Whole-cloud motion (no neighbourhood): travel; its exact split into a common translation, a radial-fit term and a remainder; how much of it coarse linear maps of the whole cloud predict on a held-out half of the images.
  • Graph quantities on the 128 nearest neighbours in the source frame: edge log-stretch and Green strain of the neighbour edges; neighbour retention (which also uses the target frame's neighbour sets); q (how differently neighbours move, relative to their separation); the budget and held-out score of a local linear fit; and capture fractions, the share of the displacement in the leading local principal directions, against a global-plane and a shuffled control. Edge quantities need only distances, so they are also measured across the shortcut.
  • Sampled or partial: the stretch profile uses 500 anchors against all images; the local fit and capture fractions exist on 8 of the 39 training intervals; the audit varies k from 32 to 1,024 on 500 anchors per frame.
  • Not measured: the tangent / normal components of the motion, the strain tensor and the rotation of local planes, because no anchor passed the reproducible-plane gate (finding 1). Second-order quantities were deferred from the start.

Two readings hold throughout. "Local" means at the radius of 128 neighbours, which at the final checkpoint is the scale of the whole cloud (earlier in training the mean distance to the 128th neighbour is smaller in some frames, down to 0.38 R on the depth clock). And the totals are means (medians for held-out scores) over the fixed population of the 10,000 test images; where subsets are used (thinning, on the audit page) the spread is a sensitivity to sample size, not a confidence interval.

Where to look

The Explorer draws the cloud at every site and measured checkpoint, coloured by a selection of the per-image fields, with playback through depth and training. It shows each stage on three fixed principal axes, and those show only part of the cloud: 6%–58% of each frame's squared spread around the reference centroid and at most 37% of a within-stage step's displacement energy, both at the final checkpoint. The components of the measured displacements outside those axes are included in every summary. The Experiments tab has the three pages behind the findings: the scale audit and calibration, motion through depth, and motion over training.

What changed

The first version of this site measured the cloud after a logit lens, as class distributions in the Fisher (Hellinger) geometry of the output. That space has only 9 dimensions for 10 classes, and at the resolution of the test set the cloud filled it, so the split into motion along and across the cloud and its strain were undefined or could not be recovered there. On 2026-11-24 the owner withdrew the Fisher readout and asked for the geometry to be measured in the raw activation space instead. Those three experiments are kept, marked deprecated, under the Experiments tab; nothing on this page depends on them.
