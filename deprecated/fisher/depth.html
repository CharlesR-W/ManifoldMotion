Motion through depth in the trained network

How does the trained ResNet-20 (training step 64,000) move the 10,000 CIFAR-10 test images through Fisher space from one block to the next?

Final checkpoint, seed 0; nine depth steps (stem→b1 to b8→b9); neighbourhoods of k = 256 images with an adaptive Gaussian kernel. Robustness checks at the end of the page.

Answer. At the resolution 10,000 images give, the lens cloud at every site from the stem to b8 has no stable thin tangent space: its local second moment decays over all nine directions without a gap. The split of motion into a part along the data manifold and a part normal to it, which the project set out to measure, is therefore not defined here. What was measured instead:

Without any fit, on every image. The last block moves images furthest (kinetic energy 1.50 for that block, 6.5 times the block before it). Neighbour distances grow at every block but the last; at b8→b9 their geometric-mean ratio is 1/9.4, and 71% of images end within Fisher–Rao distance 0.05 of a class vertex.

For the cloud as a whole. One rotation of the sphere carries 0.89 and 0.90 of the kinetic energy at b1→b2 and b2→b3, but 0.05 at b8→b9.

On images whose nine directions are resolved (99.60% to 99.91%). At the neighbourhood radius, a local linear map of the neighbourhood accounts for a minority of how neighbours move relative to each other (median slope fraction per image 0.13 to 0.47).

No stable thin directions at this resolution

For each image, the local second moment is the uncentred, graph-weighted second moment of the offsets from the image to its neighbours (sphere log maps), in the nine-dimensional tangent space of the sphere. A thin submanifold of dimension $m$, resolved at this scale, would show $m$ large eigenvalues and a gap below them. No site shows a gap. At b7 the median eigenvalues, relative to the largest, are 1, .80, .67, .50, .33, .22, .14, .08, .05. The typical image also sits near the edge of its own neighbourhood: the median one-sidedness (distance to the neighbours' weighted mean, in units of their spread) is 0.82 to 0.86 across sites.

Under the release rules of the original plan, a tangent-dependent quantity needs a stable retained subspace with a clear eigengap. Those rules pass on at most 0.07% of images at any depth step. Without a resolved tangent space there is no normal space either, so the split of velocity into tangential and normal parts, and the strain of a submanifold, are not defined here. This is the first result of the project; the rest of the page reports what was measured instead.

The replacement is the flow reading: the cloud is treated as full-dimensional, all nine directions are kept, and an image is withheld from fitted quantities where they are not all resolved. Every regression quantity is then the gain of a graph-weighted linear predictor of neighbour offsets at the neighbourhood radius, not a pointwise derivative.

How far images move, and how much of it is one rotation

The kinetic energy of a step is the mean over images of $|v/\Delta t|^2/2$, where $v = \log_{X_l} X_{l+1}$ is the displacement on the sphere of radius 2 and $\Delta t$ is one block, so it is a squared Fisher–Rao distance per squared block. The rigid part is the least-squares rotation field of the whole sphere, $v \approx \Omega x$ with $\Omega$ antisymmetric, fitted to all images at once; the rigid fraction is its share of the total kinetic energy, one number for the whole cloud.

How neighbours move relative to each other

Relative motion at an image is the weighted mean squared difference between its displacement and its neighbours' displacements (each carried to it along the sphere), divided by twice the kernel time. It is measured directly, with no fit, on 99.98% or more of images. A weighted linear predictor with a free constant term splits it exactly into three parts: a shift shared by the neighbours (the constant), a slope (the part a linear map of the neighbour offsets explains, in sample) and a residual (the rest). The split is computed on the images whose nine directions are all resolved (99.60% to 99.91% of images at this checkpoint), and there the three parts add up to the total to within a relative error of 2.15×10⁻¹⁶. Because relative motion is per unit kernel time, its absolute value depends on the bandwidth and the sample size; only the fractions compare across sites, scales and seeds.

A held-out score checks whether the linear predictor generalises within a neighbourhood: with five folds of neighbours, it is one minus the squared prediction error of next-site offsets over their squared size. It is not a centred $R^2$, and on the calibration fixture it scored above the known-map reference once there was scatter, so it is read against two baselines: no deformation (each offset carried along unchanged) and a constant offset (every offset predicted by the training neighbours' mean). The fitted score needs all nine directions resolved; the baselines do not.

Neighbour distances, and concentration near vertices

A fit-free measure of local spreading is the mean, over an image's 16 nearest neighbours, of $\log(d_{l+1}/d_l)$: the log change of each neighbour distance from one site to the next. Its exponential is the geometric-mean ratio of neighbour distances, not a factor shared by every pair.

Strain-type fields

Four fields are statistics of the fitted slope of the free-intercept predictor: the largest log gain, the expansion rate (trace of the symmetric part of the fitted displacement gradient), the strain rate and the spin (squared norms of its symmetric and antisymmetric parts). They are released only where the fit passes gates fixed on the calibration fixture: a held-out score of at least 0.5, at least 0.5 above the constant-offset score, a centred-design condition number of at most 50 and enough effective neighbours. The largest gain is gated on the prediction of next-site offsets and must also beat the no-deformation score by 0.3, unless that score is itself at least 0.95; the three rate fields are gated on the held-out prediction of displacement differences. At the final checkpoint the largest gain passes on at most 1.03% of images at any step. The expansion rate, strain rate and spin pass on at most 0.04% before b8→b9 and on 10% at b8→b9, where the mean expansion rate over the released images is −7.9 per block. The volume change $\log J$, the smallest stretches, Hencky strain and the split into volumetric and shear strain are not released at all, because the calibration showed they cannot be recovered here (see the calibration page).

Robustness

Five random subsamples of 5,000 images (each drawn without replacement, with neighbourhoods rebuilt on each) give standard deviations of at most 0.009 for the median fractions and scores, 0.0085 for the kinetic energy (at b8→b9, where it is 1.50) and 0.004 for the rigid fraction.
