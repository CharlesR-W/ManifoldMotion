Motion over training

How does the motion through depth change as the network trains, and how fast does each site's cloud move from one checkpoint to the next?

Seed 0; the 40 checkpoints the site includes (every step to 16, then doubling to 1,024, then 1,280 and 16 later checkpoints at irregular intervals to 64,000); same measurements and neighbourhoods as the depth page.

Answer. The statistics of the b8→b9 step change most over training. Its rigid fraction of the kinetic energy falls from {{f:tr.rigid.first}} at initialisation to {{f:tr.rigid.last}}; its kinetic energy, after dropping from {{f:tr.ke.first}} to {{f:tr.ke.min}} in the first steps, rises to {{f:tr.ke.last}}; the share of images it brings near a vertex is at most {{f:tr.vertex.pre1024}} before step 1,024 and reaches {{f:tr.vertex.last}}; and its mean neighbour log change turns negative (at every checkpoint after step {{f:tr.nn.lastpos}}). These are statistics of the step from b8 to b9; they do not say whether the change comes from the last block's weights or from upstream. On the training clock, all ten sites reach their highest kinetic energy (distance² per step²) within the first {{f:tc.peak.latest}} training steps, and from the first interval to the last it falls by a factor of {{f:tc.ratio.min}} to {{f:tc.ratio.max}}, depending on the site.

Where the strain-type fields are released

The training clock

On the training clock each site is held fixed and each image's lens point moves from one included checkpoint to the next. The displacement $v$ is divided by the number of training steps $\Delta t$ in the interval, so the kinetic energy $|v/\Delta t|^2/2$ is per training step squared. Intervals grow from 1 step to {{f:tc.dt.last}} steps.
