What can be measured in a nine-dimensional blob: calibration

Which of the planned kinematic quantities can a graph estimator recover from a cloud shaped like the real lens clouds, and how should the released ones be read?

A synthetic fixture matched to the lens cloud at b7, under maps whose derivatives are known exactly; two fixture seeds; the same estimator, neighbourhood size (k = 256) and kernel as the real run. The gates and tolerances below were frozen before the 40-checkpoint run.

Answer. In a fixture shaped like the real cloud, four statistics of the fitted slope (the largest log gain, the expansion rate, the strain rate and the spin) are recovered within their tolerances where gates on held-out prediction pass, provided the fit has a free intercept. The smallest stretches, the volume change $\log J$ and everything that depends on them are not: once the endpoints carry scatter, a map that collapses one direction completely is not flagged as rank-deficient. The relative-motion budget is a different kind of quantity, an in-sample decomposition released as measured rather than a gated recovery of the map. The held-out score runs above its known-map reference under scatter and is read only against its baselines. The lens passes three exact checks at all {{f:lens.n}} checkpoints.

The matched fixture

The source cloud is a nine-dimensional anisotropic Gaussian blob on the same sphere, built so that its local second-moment spectrum, neighbourhood radius, one-sidedness and kernel truncation resemble the lens cloud at b7. A smooth one-to-one map with known differential moves it; independent scatter is added to every image's endpoint at four levels (0.3, 0.6, 1.0 and 1.5, in units of the mapped cloud's median neighbourhood radius divided by $\sqrt{18}$). Contrasts use the same source: targets shuffled between images, a rank-8 map (one direction collapsed exactly), a near-singular map, the weakest direction carrying the strongest compression, a collapse towards simplex vertices, a fold, a strongly curved map, the identity and a rotation.

A fit through the image itself is biased

The planned estimator regressed each neighbour's next-site offset on its present offset with the line forced through the image itself (the anchor). Endpoint scatter moves the anchor, and that movement is shared by all of its neighbours' offsets. A forced fit absorbs this shared shift into the slope; a fit with a free constant term does not.

A diagnostic comparison on the real cloud shows the same direction. At stem→b1 in the trained network, on the images where both are defined, the forced fit attributes {{f:anch.fit.stem}} of the relative motion to its fitted linear part, while the released free-intercept slope accounts for {{f:slope.share.stem}}. The forced-fit numbers are kept in the files only as diagnostics (diag:anchored: keys) and are not results.

The held-out score is optimistic

The held-out score is one minus the squared error of predicting held-out neighbours' next-site offsets, over their squared size. On the fixture a reference score can be computed from the known map, using its numerical derivative in place of the fitted map. It is a reference, not a ceiling: on the clean smooth map the fit scores {{f:fx.ho.clean}} against a reference of {{f:fx.or.clean}}.

The score also cannot tell a deformation from a shared shift. With the targets shuffled between images there is no map at all, yet the fit scores {{f:fx.shuf.ho}}, while predicting every offset by one constant vector scores {{f:fx.shuf.const}}. On the clean smooth map the no-deformation baseline scores {{f:fx.clean.id}} (worse than predicting zero offset), because the map does deform. The released scores are therefore always printed next to both baselines, and the gates require the fit to beat the constant offset by 0.5.

What cannot be recovered, and why

Small stretches are hardest to estimate along weakly sampled directions, where the local spectrum above is lowest: there, scatter in the endpoints is hard to tell apart from stretch.

The released strain-type fields

A released value counts as false when it differs from the truth by more than the tolerance: 0.1 for the largest log gain, 0.2 of the median expansion scale for the expansion rate, and 20% of the cloud median for the strain rate and spin. Coverage is the share of points passing the release rule recorded for each fixture run.

One gate exists because of a control. On the identity map with scatter, a cloud that does not deform, scatter inflates the largest gain: with only the free-intercept gates it is released on {{f:fx.idn.pre.cov.s0}} of points (seed 1: {{f:fx.idn.pre.cov.s1}}) and {{f:fx.idn.pre.fr.s0}} of those values are outside tolerance (seed 1: {{f:fx.idn.pre.fr.s1}}). Requiring the fit to beat the no-deformation score by {{f:gate.identity_gain_min}} cuts the release to {{f:fx.idn.cov.s0}} of points on seed 0 and {{f:fx.idn.cov.s1}} on seed 1; the few values still released there remain mostly outside tolerance ({{f:fx.idn.fr.s0}} and {{f:fx.idn.fr.s1}}). Review of the measurement code

After the first run, the measurement code was reviewed independently. The review found five mismatches in release masks and labels, which were fixed, and every export was regenerated. The files behind this site, the explorer and the other pages are the regenerated ones.

Checks on the lens

Three exact checks run at every checkpoint: the lens at the last site equals ordinary inference; every lens equals an explicit forward pass with the later residual branches set to zero; and the increment identity $z_{l+1} - z_l = A_{l+1}[\mathrm{relu}(Q h_l + F(h_l)) - Q h_l]$ holds across both stage changes. All checks pass at {{f:lens.passed}} {{f:lens.n}} checkpoints; the largest absolute error anywhere is {{f:lens.worst}}.
