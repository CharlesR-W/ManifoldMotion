ManifoldMotion

The CIFAR-10 test set as a cloud of points moving through Fisher space, block by block and over training

Open the explorer

The question

A residual network changes its representation of an image a little at each block. If each intermediate representation is read as a distribution over the ten classes, every image becomes a point in one fixed space, the same at every depth and every training step. The 10,000 CIFAR-10 test images then form a cloud that moves through that space as depth increases and as training proceeds. The project set out to measure that motion as the kinematics of a deforming body: how fast each point moves, whether it slides along the cloud or carries the cloud with it, how differently neighbouring points move, and how the cloud stretches, shears and tilts. The first finding is that the cloud, at the resolution the test set gives, has no stable thin directions, so the second and last of these are not defined.

The lens and Fisher space

The network is a ResNet-20 trained on CIFAR-10 (seed {{SEED}}; {{N_ALL_STEPS}} checkpoints over {{LAST_STEP}} training steps, of which the explorer includes {{N_STEPS}}). Its residual stream is read at ten sites $l = 0, \dots, 9$: the stem output and the outputs of blocks b1 to b9. At each site a logit lens maps the activation $h_l$ to logits

$$ z_l = A_l h_l + b, $$

where $A_l$ continues along the skip path only (the shortcut's subsampling and zero channels at stage changes, then the network's own global average pool and final linear layer). So $z_l$ is what the network would output if every later residual branch were zero; at the last site it is the ordinary output.

The class distribution $p_l = \operatorname{softmax}(z_l)$ is mapped to $X_l = 2\sqrt{p_l}$ (square root taken per class), a point on the positive part of the sphere of radius 2 in $\mathbb{R}^{10}$. The metric this sphere inherits from $\mathbb{R}^{10}$ is the Fisher–Rao metric on distributions, so the distance between two images along the sphere is their Fisher–Rao distance:

$$ \begin{aligned} d(X, X') &= 4 \arctan \frac{\lVert X - X' \rVert}{\lVert X + X' \rVert} \\ &= 2 \arccos \sum_c \sqrt{p_c \, p'_c}. \end{aligned} $$

Two confident predictions of different classes are $\pi$ apart. The space is the same at every site and checkpoint, so every change of distance within the cloud is a deformation of the cloud, not of the space.

Findings

All at the final checkpoint (step {{LAST_STEP}}) unless stated, on all 10,000 test images unless a share is given.

  1. At the resolution 10,000 images give, the lens cloud has no stable thin tangent space. At every site from the stem to b8, the local second moment of the cloud (the uncentred weighted second moment of each image's offsets to its neighbours) decays smoothly over all nine directions of the sphere with no gap (at b7, relative to the largest: {{f:b7.spectrum}}). The measurement the project set out to make, the split of each image's motion into a part along the data manifold and a part normal to it, and the strain energy of that manifold, is therefore not available for this cloud: the rules that would release it pass on at most {{f:strict.tangent.max}} of images. This does not prove that no lower-dimensional structure exists; it says none is resolved at this sample size. Depth experiment.
  2. Early blocks mostly turn the cloud; the last block mostly reshapes it. For the cloud as a whole, a single rotation field of the sphere carries {{f:rigid.1}} and {{f:rigid.2}} of the kinetic energy at b1→b2 and b2→b3, but {{f:rigid.8}} at b8→b9. That last block also moves images furthest (kinetic energy {{f:ke.8}} distance² per block², against {{f:ke.7}} at b7→b8). Explorer: non-rigid speed at b8.
  3. Neighbourhoods spread at every block and contract at the last. The mean log change of distances to the 16 nearest neighbours is {{f:nn.min_early}} to {{f:nn.max_early}} per block from the stem to b8, then {{f:nn.8}} at b8→b9, a geometric-mean distance ratio of 1/{{f:nn.factor}}, as the cloud concentrates near the class vertices: {{f:vertex.last}} of images end within Fisher–Rao distance 0.05 of one. Explorer: neighbour log change at b8.
  4. Most relative motion of neighbours is not reproduced by a local linear map. On the images whose nine directions are resolved ({{f:fit9.min}} to {{f:fit9.max}}), the part of the neighbours' relative motion that a linear map of the neighbourhood explains, in sample and at the neighbourhood radius, is the minority (median per image {{f:slopemed.min}} to {{f:slopemed.max}} across depth steps); the rest is a shift shared by the neighbours and a residual. Depth experiment.
  5. Strain-type quantities are released in a few places, and volume change nowhere. Four statistics of the fitted slope (largest log gain, expansion rate, strain rate, spin) pass their calibrated gates on few images: the largest gain on at most {{f:cov.gain.max}} at any step, the other three on at most {{f:cov.E.max_early}} before b8→b9 and on {{f:cov.E.last}} at b8→b9 (mean expansion rate there {{f:E.last}} per block). A fixture matched to the real cloud showed that the smallest stretches and the volume change $\log J$ cannot be recovered at all. Calibration.
  6. The b8→b9 step's statistics change most over training. Its rigid fraction falls from {{f:tr.rigid.first}} at initialisation to {{f:tr.rigid.last}}, and the share of images it brings near a vertex rises from at most {{f:tr.vertex.pre1024}} before step 1,024 to {{f:tr.vertex.last}}. On the training clock, every site reaches its highest kinetic energy (distance² per step²) within the first {{f:tc.peak.latest}} steps, and from the first interval to the last it falls by a factor of {{f:tc.ratio.min}} to {{f:tc.ratio.max}}. Training experiment.

A second seed shows the same last block (kinetic energy {{f:seed1.ke.last}}, rigid fraction {{f:seed1.rigid.last}}, neighbour log change {{f:seed1.nn.last}}); halving or doubling the neighbourhood size moves the median slope and shift fractions by at most {{f:scale.frac.change}}.

What was measured instead

The images are the material points, and $X_l(x)$ is image $x$'s position at site $l$. The displacement from site $l$ to $l+1$ is the tangent vector $v_l(x) = \log_{X_l(x)} X_{l+1}(x)$ of the sphere; its length is the Fisher–Rao distance travelled. There are two clocks: depth (ten positions at one checkpoint) and training (one site across checkpoints, rates per training step). Kinetic energy is $|v/\Delta t|^2/2$, a squared distance per squared unit of time. Because no thin directions are resolved, local quantities use the flow reading: the cloud is treated as full-dimensional, and every regression quantity is the gain of a graph-weighted linear predictor of neighbour offsets at the neighbourhood radius (k = 256 nearest neighbours), not a pointwise derivative.

  • On every image, with no fit: speed and kinetic energy; the mean log change of neighbour distances; the distance to the nearest class vertex; relative motion of neighbours and the "no deformation" and "constant offset" prediction scores (these need only a neighbourhood: {{f:relm.mass.min}} or more of images).
  • For the cloud as a whole: the share of kinetic energy carried by one global rotation field of the sphere.
  • Where all nine directions are resolved ({{f:fit9.min}} to {{f:fit9.max}} of images at the final checkpoint, at least {{f:fit9.min.all}} at any measured checkpoint): the exact split of relative motion into a shift shared by the neighbours, a slope explained by a linear map (in sample) and a residual; the held-out score of the linear fit, always read against the two baselines.
  • Where calibrated gates pass (few images): the largest log gain, the expansion rate, the strain rate and the spin of the fitted map.
  • Not released: the tangential / normal split, strain of a submanifold, the smallest stretches, Hencky strain and $\log J$.

Neighbourhoods are built with the carré du champ (Bakry–Émery) calculus on a weighted graph in which each image carries mass $1/N$. Every average states the share of images it is defined on.

What would change this

More test images would help little: in nine dimensions the neighbourhood radius at a fixed number of neighbours shrinks only like $N^{-1/9}$, so halving it needs about 500 times more points. A readout with fewer dimensions, or an analysis restricted to one class or to the images near a vertex, might find thin directions that the pooled cloud does not have. The 50,000 training images were deliberately left for a later comparison.
